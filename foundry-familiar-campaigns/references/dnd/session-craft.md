# D&D 5e session craft

Extra checks for D&D 5e content. Use these alongside [Session prep](../session-prep.md). They're techniques, not a formula: the table's preferences win.

## Edition first

Find out whether the table plays **2014**, **2024**, or a mix. Check the dnd5e system version and the Table Profile, and ask if it's unclear and it matters. Familiar's combat resolvers implement 2024 rules.

A 2014 adventure runs fine under 2024 rules, but verify these per encounter rather than assuming:

- stat blocks: the 2024 *Monster Manual* versions differ in HP, actions and damage
- surprise: 2024 gives disadvantage on initiative instead of a lost turn
- exhaustion: 2024 uses a −2-per-level d20 penalty instead of the 2014 table
- spells and class features named in the text (several changed in 2024)
- weapon mastery (2024 only)

Rules references (free): [2024 playing the game](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/playing-the-game), [classes](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/character-classes), [equipment](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/equipment), [DM's toolbox](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/dms-toolbox). Quote the rule you rely on. Don't work from memory of an older ruling.

Imported sheet data and Familiar's helper text are evidence of *implementation*, not proof of the rules. If they disagree with the rule text, the rule text wins; note the mismatch for the GM.

## Solo and small-party safety

XP budgets lie for one character. For each fight, write down:

- the enemies' best single-round damage against the PC's HP (a crit included)
- what happens if the sidekick or companion drops first
- whether the PC can escape, negotiate or avoid the fight, and what the source says about that
- the action economy: one PC against several attackers is far deadlier than the numbers suggest

Keep the source's danger unless the GM approves a change (record it in Adaptations). Easy wins are fine when earlier choices earned them. Don't inflate a fight to rescue pacing.

## Making the opening runnable

Before calling an opening ready, privately confirm:

- **Place:** what's physically here, what can be examined or used, and what searches find
- **People:** wants, knowledge limits, and responses to friendly, pushy or hostile approaches
- **Discovery:** what can be learned here and the decision it leads to
- **Change:** what develops if the player waits or acts, with visible warning signs
- **Decisions:** concrete options with tradeoffs, not just "talk / search / leave"

Write actual content, not instructions to write it later. "Add a clue" or "reward appropriately" are failures. Fantasy should *do* something in a location, not only decorate it. A deliberately quiet scene can stay quiet.

## Gamebook and DM-free adventures

Solo gamebooks (numbered entries, codewords, "turn to 47") assume the player is their own GM. With an AI GM:

- The AI narrates the entry. It may paraphrase delivery, but choices, checks, DCs and outcomes stay exact.
- Offer the entry's listed choices. If the player proposes something else sensible, rule fairly from the fiction, log the ruling in Adaptations, and steer by the fiction (not by refusal) back toward entries that exist.
- "Turn to N" targets are secret until reached. Never print the entry number of an unvisited outcome.
- Codewords go in Trackers the moment they're earned, with the entry that granted them.
- Rest restrictions and combat-sheet special rules are source rules. Put them on the Runner Guide or the encounter's prep line so they aren't forgotten.

## Design lessons worth borrowing

Original summaries of public advice; sources reviewed September 2026.

| Source | Takeaway | Don't over-apply |
|---|---|---|
| [Phandalin guidance](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1515-visit-phandalin-d-ds-most-popular-starter-town) | A home base whose people notice the party's deeds makes hooks feel earned | Not every resident is a quest board |
| [Running Icespire Peak](https://slyflourish.com/running_icespire_peak.html) | Small, clear jobs; small parties meet badly mismatched threats | Published level ranges don't guarantee safety |
| [First Blush](https://dndduet.com/first-blush/) | A duet adventure gives one PC's abilities room to matter | Don't force a tutorial on an experienced player |
| [Three Clue Rule](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1118/roleplaying-games/three-clue-rule) | Several independent routes to each essential conclusion | Three is a heuristic, not a quota |
| [Run Simple Adventures](https://slyflourish.com/run_simple_adventures.html) | A clear situation, and let complexity come from choices | Simple doesn't mean empty |
